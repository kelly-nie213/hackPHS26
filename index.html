How it works

Every sunny hour of a real year, counted

Zoning lets towers rise in exchange for public benefits like housing. The cost, a shadow on everyone else, is rarely measured. We measure it, building by building.

  1. Replay the weather

    We take every hour of the year the sun actually shone, from historical cloud and radiation data, and find where the sun was in the sky.

  2. Cast every shadow

    For each of those hours we cast every building's shadow and measure the public ground it covers: streets, sidewalks, plazas and parks. Rooftops don't count.

  3. ACF

    Divide by homes

    Shade per home shows whether a tower's shadow bought housing. Buildings are graded A–F against their neighbors. A slim luxury spire with few homes scores worst.

Data & caveats

Sources

  • New York: NYC Open Data building footprints (surveyed roof heights) and PLUTO tax lots (actual homes per lot). Parks from NYC Parks.
  • Other cities: OpenStreetMap buildings, tower parts and parks. Heights come from mapped heights or floor counts.
  • Weather: Open-Meteo historical archive, with hourly sunshine duration, cloud cover and radiation.

Keep in mind

  • Outside New York, home counts are estimated from building type and floor area unless OpenStreetMap lists the number of units. Mixed-use towers may show no homes.
  • Buildings with no mapped height are drawn at a typical low-rise height.
  • Only towers inside each study area are counted, so parks at the edges are undercounted.
